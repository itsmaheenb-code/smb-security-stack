Multi-factor authentication is the single highest-value security control most small businesses haven't fully rolled out — it blocks the majority of account-takeover attacks even when a password leaks. The question isn't whether to use it, it's which method fits your team and budget.

Free authenticator apps

For a very small team, Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator costs nothing and covers the core benefit of MFA: a password alone is no longer enough to log in. What you give up is centralized administration — there's no dashboard to see who has MFA enabled, enforce it, or revoke access remotely.

Cisco Duo

Duo is built to work across a mixed environment — it doesn't require standardizing on one identity provider — and adds device posture checks (is this device up to date and secure?), a verified push option, and anomaly detection on top of basic MFA. It's a strong choice once you want admin visibility without being locked into Microsoft's identity stack.

Microsoft Entra MFA

If your team already runs on Microsoft 365, Entra MFA (formerly Azure AD MFA) is usually the path of least resistance — Entra ID P1 is included in Microsoft 365 E3, or available standalone, with P2 adding risk-based conditional access for teams that need it. Since you're already managing identity through Microsoft, this avoids adding a second vendor into the mix.

YubiKey hardware keys

Push notifications and SMS codes can be phished — an employee can be tricked into approving a fraudulent login. Hardware keys using FIDO2/ WebAuthn can't be phished the same way, which is why they're the recommended baseline for admins, finance roles, and anyone with access to sensitive systems, even on a team that uses a simpler method for everyone else. At $50-100 per key, it's a one-time cost rather than a subscription.

Which one should you pick?

Very small team, no budget: free authenticator apps for everyone. Growing team that wants admin control without full Microsoft lock-in: Duo. Already on Microsoft 365: Entra MFA. Whatever you choose for the rest of the team, add YubiKeys for admins and finance — the highest-risk accounts are worth the extra step regardless of what everyone else uses.

FAQ

Do I need a paid MFA tool, or is a free authenticator app enough?

For a very small team, a free authenticator app covers the core security benefit at $0. Paid tools like Duo or Entra MFA add centralized admin control, device posture checks, and enforced policies across the team — worth it once you're managing MFA for more than a handful of people.

What is phishing-resistant MFA and do I need it?

SMS codes and push notifications can be phished or socially engineered (a user tricked into approving a fraudulent login). Phishing-resistant MFA — hardware keys like YubiKey using FIDO2/WebAuthn — can't be phished this way. It's worth the extra cost for admins, finance roles, and anyone with access to sensitive systems, even if the rest of the team uses a simpler method.

Should I use Microsoft Entra MFA or a third-party tool like Duo?

If your team is already on Microsoft 365, Entra MFA is often the simplest choice since it's built into identity you're already managing. Duo is a stronger fit for a mixed environment — a team not fully standardized on Microsoft, or one that wants MFA decoupled from a single identity vendor.

What happens if an employee loses their hardware security key?

This is why every MFA rollout needs backup authentication methods configured per user (a second registered key, a backup code, or a fallback app) before it's needed — plan for lost-key recovery during setup, not after someone is locked out.
