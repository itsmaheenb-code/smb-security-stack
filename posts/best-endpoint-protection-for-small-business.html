FAQ

What's the difference between EPP, EDR, and MDR?

EPP (endpoint protection platform) is traditional antivirus — it blocks known malware before it runs. EDR (endpoint detection and response) adds behavioral monitoring to catch threats that get past prevention, plus tools to investigate and respond. MDR (managed detection and response) adds a human team that watches those EDR alerts for you, for businesses without in-house security staff.

Do I already have endpoint protection if I use Microsoft 365?

If you're on Microsoft 365 Business Premium or E5, Microsoft Defender for Business is already included at no extra cost — check your current plan before paying for a separate tool. Lower Microsoft 365 tiers don't include it and would need it added separately.

Is CrowdStrike overkill for a 10-person business?

Often, yes, unless you're specifically required to have EDR for a cyber insurance policy, a customer security questionnaire, or a compliance requirement. For most small teams without that specific requirement, Microsoft Defender for Business or Malwarebytes covers the practical risk at a fraction of the cost.

Why do endpoint security prices vary so much between sources?

Vendors in this space price by tier (EPP vs. EDR vs. MDR), by commitment length, and often negotiate per-deal for business customers — so publicly quoted numbers vary more than in simpler software categories. Always confirm current pricing directly with the vendor for your team size before deciding.
