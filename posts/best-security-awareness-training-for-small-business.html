KnowBe4

~$1.30–2.35/user/month

KnowBe4 is the category's most established name, with four tiers (Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond) that add multi-channel phishing simulations, SIEM-integrated reporting, and eventually AI-driven tools as you move up. List pricing runs $1.30-$2.35/user/month, but negotiated rates are commonly 20-50% below list depending on team size and contract length — always ask for a quote rather than assuming list price applies.

Want the deepest content library and most mature reporting, and can negotiate on price: KnowBe4. Already run Huntress for endpoint protection and want one vendor: Huntress SAT. Small team or just starting out and want to try training before committing budget: Wizer's free tier, upgrading to Boost once you need phishing simulations.

FAQ

Is free security awareness training actually good enough?

For a very small team, a free tier like Wizer's covers the fundamentals — basic training videos and quizzes with no seat minimum. What you give up versus a paid tool is automated phishing simulations and detailed per-employee risk reporting, which matter more once you have enough staff that you can't track behavior informally.

What happened to Curricula?

Curricula was acquired by Huntress in 2022 for $22 million and has since been integrated into Huntress's own security awareness training product, now sold as Huntress Managed SAT rather than under the Curricula name.

Do I need phishing simulations specifically, or is training content enough?

Training content teaches people what phishing looks like in theory; simulated phishing emails test whether that knowledge holds up in a real inbox moment. The gap between the two is often large — simulations are what actually change click-through behavior, not just quiz scores.

Is this worth it if my team already went through general "stay safe online" training once?

A one-time session has a short half-life — phishing tactics evolve and awareness fades within months without reinforcement. Ongoing, periodic training (most of these tools run on a monthly or quarterly cadence) is what keeps the effect from decaying, not a single onboarding session.
