Google Drive / OneDrive (your existing plan)

$0 extra

Worth saying plainly: if you're already on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, you're already paying for Drive or OneDrive storage, and it's genuinely fine for the large majority of business files. Google and Microsoft encrypt data in transit and at rest and offer solid access controls, admin logging, and (on higher-tier plans) data-loss-prevention features. What they don't offer is zero-knowledge encryption — Google and Microsoft hold the encryption keys, which means they technically could access file contents (for legal compliance, account recovery, etc.), even if they don't do so routinely. That distinction doesn't matter for most files. It matters a lot for the specific subset — regulated data, signed legal documents, anything under a strict confidentiality clause — that this post is really about.

Which one should you pick?

Handling client health records, signed legal agreements, or anything where you need to be able to say "not even our storage provider can read this": Tresorit, for the more clearly documented compliance paperwork. Want real end-to-end encryption for a smaller sensitive-document folder without Tresorit's price tag, and don't need a confirmed HIPAA BAA: Sync.com. Storing ordinary day-to-day business files that aren't under any particular confidentiality or regulatory obligation: stick with the Google Drive or OneDrive storage you're already paying for and save the extra line item for the files that actually need it. Most small businesses end up running both — Drive or OneDrive for everything routine, and a small, clearly-labeled zero-knowledge vault for the handful of documents that genuinely warrant it.

FAQ

What does "zero-knowledge encryption" actually mean?

It means the files are encrypted on your device before they're uploaded, and the provider never holds the key to decrypt them — so even if their servers were breached or a staff member were compelled to hand over data, your files would be unreadable. Standard Google Drive and OneDrive encrypt files in transit and at rest, but Google and Microsoft hold the keys, so it's not zero-knowledge.

Isn't Google Drive or OneDrive secure enough already?

For most day-to-day business files, yes — both use strong encryption and access controls, and most small businesses never need more. The gap is specifically for documents where you need to guarantee that not even the storage provider can access the contents: signed contracts, client PHI or financial records, M&A due diligence, or anything under a strict confidentiality obligation.

Do I need this if I already have a password manager and MFA?

They solve different problems. A password manager and MFA protect who can log in to an account; zero-knowledge storage protects the files themselves even if an account, a server, or the provider's own infrastructure is compromised. Most small businesses with sensitive-document needs end up using both, not one instead of the other.

Which one has a HIPAA BAA available?

Tresorit publishes HIPAA compliance information and offers a Business Associate Agreement on its business plans, which is the clearer documented option of the two for handling PHI. We could not confirm from public sources whether Sync.com currently offers a signed BAA — if you need one, get written confirmation from each vendor's sales team before you store any protected health information.
