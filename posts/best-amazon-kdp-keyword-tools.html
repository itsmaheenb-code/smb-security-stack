Keyword and category research decides whether your book shows up when someone searches Amazon, or sits invisible next to a million other titles. Here's the paid option most indie authors eventually try, and what you can do for free before spending anything.

Publisher Rocket

Publisher Rocket (built by Kindlepreneur) is a one-time-purchase desktop tool — no subscription — that pulls estimated search volume for Amazon keywords, surfaces category and competition data, and helps you find the "also boughts" and comparable titles for your ad targeting. List price is $199, though discounts bringing it closer to $97–$120 show up regularly, so it's worth checking for a current promo before paying full price.

It pays for itself fastest around a launch, when picking the right categories and keywords directly affects whether your book gets an Amazon bestseller tag in a low-competition category — which then feeds its own visibility.

What you can do for free first

  • Amazon autocomplete: type a seed keyword into Amazon's search bar and note what it suggests — those are real, frequent searches.
  • Competitor "Also boughts": look at comparable titles in your genre and note which categories and keywords they're ranking under.
  • Google Keyword Planner (free with a Google Ads account): not Amazon-specific, but useful for gauging general interest in a topic or subgenre.

This gets you a reasonable starting keyword list at $0. What it can't do is estimate actual Amazon search volume or find low-competition categories systematically — that's the gap a paid tool closes.

Is it worth it?

For a single low-stakes title, the free methods are probably enough. If you're planning multiple launches, running Amazon ads, or trying to break into a specific category strategically, a one-time $97–$199 tool that removes the guesswork on every future book is a reasonable investment relative to what a single poorly-targeted ad campaign can burn through.
